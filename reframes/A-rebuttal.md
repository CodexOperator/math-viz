# Rebuttal A

Mine said: plant, turn, do not lift. The colour is the table remembering the hold. That is the right muscle and the wrong sentence for the night before Test 2. The plan opens on a sequence, where a cycle and a limit are different facts, and my top line would paint both blue.

## What B gets right that this pitch missed

The unit circle is one wall, and I never drew it as a wall. Powers that die, the geometric series, the filled set of \(z^2\), and the place where a multiplier product changes its mind are one equator. I had a finger and a stain and no latitude. "Still on the table" does not say where the table ends.

B also separates two trails I had filed as one grip. Twist covered both \(\lambda^n\) and the squaring orbit. At \((1+i)/\sqrt{2}\) those trails part: the powers cycle the eighth roots forever, and the squares go to \(i\), then \(-1\), then \(1\). The spine names them in one breath. The night-before error is treating that breath as one motion. B puts the split on the equator, where it actually happens.

The sharper point is the one I am most tempted to blur. The finger's fate and the pinch's fate are different points. For \(z^2\), the orbit falls to \(0\) for \(|z| < 1\). The one-step multiplier \(2z\) lies inside the unit disk only for \(|z| < 1/2\). On the equator the place stays and the product of the derivatives along the orbit runs outward. Definition 4.2.18 and the disk at \(c = 0\) are not the same disk. My pinch sat on the finger as if it agreed with the finger. B's product is what keeps them apart. Drop that and the page teaches a false merger, which is worse than two maps.

Infinity as a place is the same kind of correction. I said the trail leaves. B says it arrives at the north pole the reciprocal station already built. Terms walking to \(0\) and partial sums running off are then two destinations, not one orange word.

## B's fatal flaw the night before Test 2

B says a student will misread the still, and then puts the still on the page. Two poles cannot name three roots. Saying so underneath the picture does not unload the legend.

The rest of the page fights the clock. Sphere, chart, power trail, squaring trail, product disk, microscope, cardioid, seam strip, four stills. B's own cut, down to sphere and chart and one trail, is already the two-picture exhibit this site spent three batches removing. Addition, the series, Newton's subtraction, and \(|z-a|\) live in the chart, and they are half of what the student will write. A sphere that makes them look secondary teaches the wrong half of tomorrow's paper. Purple for a cycle, borrowed from the third root, spends the one spare colour on a fate the key already assigned.

## What C gets right that this pitch missed

My freezes were still rooms. A student who can hold the finger down and cannot tell the sentence's kind fails the paper.

The fate words are the part I have to take whole. Settles: eventually inside every disk around a limit (Definition 4.1.1). My "blue, still on the table" is the Julia legend worn on the sequence page, which is the first fifteen minutes. A point of \(((1+i)/\sqrt{2})^n\) stays and does not settle. ## What this pitch should absorb

Yes. The hand stays. The sentence changes.

From C, take the fate words and the beat. The first press is once. Again is a second press, capped at eight, and it does not start by itself. The line says settles, stays, or leaves, in those senses. Newton's fate is which root, and that word is not also the floor and not also the address. A series draws two marks, bags and partial sums, and states 4.1.17 in one direction. The slide \(c\) is a notch. A root is a pin. Paint, when it comes, is fate only.

From B, take the split, not the instrument. On the pure twist with the notch parked at \(0\), the finger draws two trails, powers and squares. Off the unit circle they share a fate. On it they part, and the product of the pinches along the orbit leaves while the finger stays. One clause in the line, a second polyline, no sphere, no product disk, no cardioid inset. The cardioid remains the sentence \(c = \lambda/2 - \lambda^2/4\) when the pinch is the thing being tested. Leaving means headed for the point at infinity the reciprocal page already has. The sphere and the helicoid stay the stations they are. Tonight the student has to say the equator and the floors. They do not have to learn a second view in order to say them.

Leave B's purple cycle, the side-by-side sphere as the page, and the Newton still under a hemisphere legend. Leave C's tilt as the front door, the retired threads, the preset table, and the paint of the set of \(c\).
