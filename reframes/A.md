# Reframe A — Press, and keep turning

Lens: the learner's hands. The wide review said the course in one sentence: a point has two addresses and a function moves the plane; the colour of a start is the fate of doing that motion again. That sentence is right, and it is still a map of rooms. A hand cannot hold a map. It can hold one plant on the table and the decision not to lift.

This is a pitch for one new page. It does not change the stations.

## Questions

1. When is a complex number a place the hand is in, and when is it a turn the hand can do? On Addresses, \(z\) is a plant. In multiplication the same letter is a spin and a stretch. The staircase keeps the plant fixed and changes the name of the turn. A learner holding both tests at once has to know which of those the hand is doing. "A place and a move" may be two reports of one placement, available before anything moves, or two uses that only share a letter.

2. Whose memory is the knowledge? The finger remembers a trail: the next landing, then the next. The table remembers a colour: what would have happened had the finger planted there and stayed. A sequence in §4.1 is a finger-memory. A Julia set is a table-memory. The Test 2 home shows the finger as a list of coordinates and sends the table to another stop. Which one is the learner supposed to be?

3. "Again" is two motions that feel alike. An orbit applies the same turn to the place you just landed. A series stacks a smaller copy of a turn onto a running sum. A student who has felt \(((1+i)/\sqrt{2})^n\) cycle the eighth roots of unity has not yet felt why \(\sum z^n\) settles inside the disk. Is the friendship real, both of them "the turn, counted," or does Chapter 4 glue a composition to a sum because they share a week?

4. The derivative, in Howell & Mathews and in Test 1's fourth line, is a zoom. Zooming is a motion of the head. The hand's version is a pinch: two fingers close, and the cloth between them either turns as one piece or flips. The book defines \(f'(z_0)\) as a limit of a quotient, and that quotient is itself a complex number, so it is itself a turn. If the course is one gesture, the microscope has to be felt. Does the pinch survive contact with that definition?

5. The seam is the only place the course hurts. The fingertip is back where it was. The wrist is not. \(\operatorname{Arg} z\) snaps and the point does not. Is that mismatch the lesson that makes the rest one gesture — you learn to feel the wrist apart from the dot — or is it a fact about choosing names that a hand-story will soften?

6. Section 3.3 reads a height. A pure turn-and-stretch has no hill under the palm, so the Laplacian is zero off the origin. That is a check, not a motion. The wide review called a Laplacian the wrong last idea. Should the hand ever walk there?

7. The set of \(c\) is the fate of one plant, the origin, as the other hand slides. The site states the set and does not draw it. Is that a decision about pictures, the knowledge already complete in the orbit of 0, or is the knowledge the shape?

## The pitch

The whole course is one placement of the hand and the refusal to lift it.

Plant a finger on the table. That plant is a complex number. Before any turn, you can report it in two ways. Across, then up: the Cartesian address, the slide a second hand would make to arrive here. Reach from the peg at 0, and the angle of the wrist from the positive real axis: the polar address. Both reports are true of the same finger. Test 1's first line is what you can say with the finger down and the wrist still.

A turn of the wrist, reach allowed to change, is multiplication. A slide of the palm with no twist is addition. A flip of the hand is conjugation. Turning the hand inside out, so what was far is near, is \(1/z\). Every function in Howell & Mathews, Chapters 2 to 4, is a rule for which turn the finger takes at that plant. Between two pinched fingers an analytic rule feels like one rigid twist: spin and stretch, no flip. That pinch is the derivative. Cauchy–Riemann is the pinch agreeing with itself. The quotient in the definition is the pinch, written as a complex number.

Test 2 is the same finger, not lifted. It lands, and the same turn plays from the new plant. The trail of landings is the sequence of §4.1. If instead you keep a running total of smaller and smaller turns, the trail is a series. The colour is the table's memory of a hold you did not end: blue where the trail stays on the table, orange where it leaves, and, when the turn is Newton's correction, the colour of the still-point the corrections walk to. The wide sentence and the hand are the same fact. The finger walks. The table stains. The learner is the finger. The colour is what the finger would have found at every other plant.

The staircase is that hold continued past a full circle. The fingertip repeats. The wrist has wound. \(\arg z\) is every winding that puts the finger back on that plant. \(\operatorname{Arg} z\) is the wrist returned to the comfortable range \((-\pi,\pi]\), and the return has to snap somewhere. The snap is in the wrist. The ramp — the sheet lifted into a spiral, the Riemann surface the branches page already draws — is the table agreeing to keep the wrist's count.

So the thirty-five stops are moments you are allowed to look down during one hold. A student who says it was all one thing is saying: I planted, I turned, I did not lift, and every page was a moment of that.

Three grips, chosen with the hand up, before the plant. During the hold the grip does not change.

- **Twist.** A power, a linear map, \(z^2+c\). This is the muscle you can mime with an empty hand: double the angle, square the reach, slide.
- **Correction.** One Newton step, \(z - p(z)/p'(z)\), then the same step from where you landed. This is a different muscle. The sentence names the quotient. It does not call the correction a twist.
- **Stack.** Add the next term of a series onto the running sum. "Again" here is stacking, and the sentence says so.

The gesture is the hold. The grip is which turn you hold. That is the whole reorganisation: one table, one finger, three ways the turn can be, and a colour that means you did not stop.

## The screen and the one gesture

One static page. The plane is the viewport. A single KaTeX line sits on the top edge and updates with the finger. A thin strip along the bottom names the freeze: which existing stop this moment is, as a link to that hash. No topic chips, no finder, no thread bars. The threads are ways of cutting the hold, and this page is the hold itself.

The gesture, in order, as one motion:

1. **Pointer down plants \(z\).** Drop-lines and a ray appear: the two addresses of this plant. Once the finger moves they fade to a hairline. They were a report, and they have been made. A small square at the fingertip is already turned by \(f'(z)\). The pinch is visible before any iteration. The top line reads the Cartesian address, the polar address, and the grip.

2. **Holding still starts the walk.** One landing every 90 ms, the beat the Julia film already uses. The trail is dots. The line names the step and the fate in the picture colour. Blue: the trail is still inside the disk that counts as staying. Orange: it has left. With reduced motion the line jumps to the fate after a fixed count and the trail draws once.

3. **Moving while held drags the plant.** The trail clears and starts again. This is the green point. Green is the start, as it is on the current pictures.

4. **The orange peg is the slide, and it lives on the table.** A second pointer, or a later grab of that peg with the same finger, translates the additive constant. For \(z^2+c\) the peg is \(c\). For \(az+b\) the peg is \(b\), and a turn of the peg around the plant sets \(\arg a\) and \(|a|\). During the slide the stain and the trail stay where they were, and the peg follows. Letting go replays the hold along the path the hand took. A typed number or a chip jumps, and a jump is not a path. That is the contract the Julia film already keeps. The peg and an escaped trail share orange. The line says which one it is talking about: "the peg" or "left."

5. **Winding the plant around 0 is the staircase.** The fingertip is continuous. Past the seam, \(\operatorname{Arg}\) jumps in the line. If the hold continues past that jump, a spiral ramp rises under the finger, in the Three.js view the branches station already knows, composited in the same frame as the table. The plant climbs. \(\arg\) keeps its count. You have not opened a second page.

6. **A pinch fills the screen with the local square.** Two pointers moving together, or, on a phone, a control that zooms onto the square already drawn at the finger. The line becomes \(f'(z)\) as a spin and a stretch. For \(\overline{z}\), \(\operatorname{Re} z\), and \(|z|^2\) the square flips or squashes, and the line says the pinch mirrors.

7. **Lifting freezes the moment.** The bottom strip names the stop. The stain, if it has started, stays until the next plant. A long hold may ask the existing off-thread renderer for a coarse field of fates. The field is late, and it is a record of this grip and this peg. The sharp picture remains the Julia or Newton station the strip links to.

The three example pegs the site already trusts are the three places a learner should put the orange point first: \(c = 0\), \(c = -0.75\), \(c = i\). At \(0\) the stain wants to be a disk. At \(-0.75\) the same plant stays and the disk has pinched. At \(i\) the plant at \(0.45+0.2i\) leaves, and the line turns orange.

## Where every stop lands

Each stop is a freeze of the hold. The plant, the grip, and the step you stop at are the whole difference between one page and the next.

### Test 1

**The Map.** This table. The old home, with its finder and its four lines, is the list of freezes, and it stays where it is. The four lines become four moments: the report before the turn (place and move), the wrist past a full circle (the staircase), the sheet carried one step (motion and time), the pinch (zoom).

**One Point, Two Addresses.** The plant, before step 1. Drop-lines and the ray. Problem 1.3.1 is the dot Test 1 already plants on its home page; here it is the finger at pointer-down.

**Slide, Spin, Stretch, Mirror.** Twist or slide, stopped at step 1, then lifted. A second plant is the other hand. Adding slides by it. Multiplying turns by its angle and stretches by its reach. Conjugation flips the hand. \(1/z\) turns the hand inside out. The result is where the finger sits after one application.

**Length, Distance, Circles.** The reach \(|z|\) is the ray. \(|z_1-z_2|\) is the length of the slide between two plants. A circle is every plant at one reach from a centre. The triangle inequality is the slack when two slides replace a direct one.

**The Staircase.** Winding the plant around 0. The fingertip never jumps. \(\operatorname{Arg} z\) snaps at the seam if you stay on the floor. The ramp keeps every name in \(\arg z\). The point \(z\) is the plant on the floor either way.

**Powers.** Twist grip, the same turn counted \(n\) times before you look. The spiral of dots is the trail stopped at \(n\). The final angle wrapped into \((-\pi,\pi]\) is the home wrist.

**Roots.** The plants at which a wrist divided by \(n\) lands on the same fingertip. They sit on one circle. The quadratic formula is this freeze at a square root.

**Curves and Time.** The plant moves on a clock while the turn waits. A stroke \(z(t)\), scrubbed, reversed. The segment built two ways is two slides that end on one plant.

**Disks, Annuli, Boundaries.** A small disk around the plant. Open, closed, bounded, connected, domain: each is a sentence about whether a short slide stays in the set. The annulus \(1 < |z+i| \le 3\) is a band of reaches from \(-i\).

**A Function Is a Motion.** The twist applied to every plant at once, one step, then lift. The sheet before and after. \(f(z) = z^2+iz\) is one such turn, written in both addresses, which is what the station already computes.

**Linear Maps.** The turn is the same at every plant (one \(a\)) plus one slide (\(b\)). The three stages are that turn, that stretch, and that slide, lifted between them. \(iz-2i\), the unit circle under \((1+i)z\), and a composition are three freezes of this grip.

**\(z^n\): Fans and Parabolas.** The twist counted \(n\) times across the sheet. Rays fan because the wrist is multiplied. The line \(x = 2\) becomes a parabola because the turn reads reach and angle, and a vertical line is not a ray.

**Principal Roots.** Among the \(n\) roots, the one whose wrist is the home step is solid. The others are ghost windings. Sweep the plant around 0 and the solid root jumps at the seam.

**Branches and Cuts.** Where the snap is allowed to happen. A test walk across the negative real axis jumps on the floor and continues on the ramp. The movable seam is a choice of where the wrist resets.

**\(1/z\) and the Sphere.** The inside-out turn, stopped at one step. The circle \(|z-1| = 1\) opens into the line \(\operatorname{Re} w = 1/2\). The sphere is the table wrapped so a slide that runs off the edge arrives at one point.

**Limits.** Walk the plant into a point along different strokes: lines, \(y = x^2\), a spiral. The landing has to agree. Directional values that trace a circle mean the turn has no single end, so the limit does not exist. The epsilon-delta disks are the small disk around the plant and the small disk around the landing.

**The Derivative Is a Move.** The pinch. The ring of directions and its image. The square. The line is \(f'(z_0)\), one spin-and-stretch, exactly when the pinch agrees with itself. This is Test 1's zoom, felt between two fingers and written as the book's quotient.

**Cauchy–Riemann.** The pinch read two ways. The residuals are the disagreement. The two equalities hold when the square does not flip. \(\overline{z}\), \(\operatorname{Re} z\), and \(|z|^2\) fail here, and the \(u = c\) case is a pinch with nowhere to turn.

**The Mapping Lab.** Any stroke you draw is a chain of plants carried by one turn on one clock. Side by side, morph, domain colouring, the modulus landscape, the sphere, and the 4D graph are ways of looking at that carried stroke. The lab is the hold with the turn applied to a drawing instead of to one finger.

**The 4D Graph.** The turn has a height the table does not show. Dragging to orbit the camera is a motion of the head, and the page should call it that. The real surface, the imaginary surface, the modulus landscape, and the Riemann surface are shadows of one graph. Two angles pick the shadow.

**The Problem Index.** Each card is a freeze: plant, grip, step, and the sentence the book asks. Opening a card from this page sets those and pauses, so the quotation is a hold you can resume.

### Test 2

**The Map (Test 2).** The same table. The thesis on the current home — a colour is the fate of repeating one motion — is the top line after a few steps. The three slides \(c = 0\), \(c = -0.75\), \(c = i\) are three positions of the orange peg, with the plant at \(0.45+0.2i\). The night-before plan is a suggested order of freezes, and it can keep living on the old home.

**A Sequence Has a Fate.** Twist grip, not lifted. Definition 4.1.1 is the trail eventually sitting inside every disk you draw around the limit. \((1/2+i/4)^n\) shrinks onto 0; the reach goes to 0, which is why. \(((1+i)/\sqrt{2})^n\) visits the eight roots of unity and never settles, so it has no limit. Bounded, Definition 4.1.7 and Theorem 4.1.8, means the trail fits in some disk. A trail that settles must.

**Terms Must Go to 0.** Stack grip. Theorem 4.1.17: if the stack settles, the added turns go to 0. The converse is false. A stack whose terms shrink and whose sum still runs is the thing to feel here, reaches that get shorter too slowly. \(z_n \to 0\) exactly when the reach does.

**Do the Motion Again.** The trail of the twist grip, named as an orbit: \(z\), \(f(z)\), \(f(f(z))\). One motion from Test 1, not lifted. This is the freeze where the page first says the word orbit, which is the book's word after the Newton pictures in §4.2.

**When \(c\) Is 0, a Disk.** Peg at 0, rule \(z^2\). Plants with reach at most 1 stay, because squaring shortens a reach below 1 and leaves a reach of 1 alone. The stain is the closed unit disk. The edge is the circle the roots of unity sit on. Example 4.2.8 (PDF Example 4.9). Exercise 4.2.4, the modulus \(|z_0|^{2^k}\), is this freeze with the reach read out at step \(k\).

**Julia Sets.** The peg moves. The rule is square, then slide by the peg. \(K_c\) is every plant whose trail stays bounded, and the Julia set is the edge of that stain. The film of a dragged peg is the replay on release. \(f_c(z) = z^2+c\), as the station cites. The handle is the peg. An orange pixel is a trail that left.

**The Orbit of 0.** The plant fixed at 0, the place where the pinch of \(z^2+c\) vanishes, because the derivative is \(2z\). Theorem 4.2.11 (PDF Theorem 4.9): the edge is one piece exactly when this plant's trail stays bounded. You feel it by planting at 0 and holding, peg still.

**One Newton Step.** Correction grip, said out loud. Polynomial \(z^2+1\), plant at \(1/4+i/4\), five steps, landing on \(i\). Example 4.2.1 (PDF Example 4.7). A line needs one step because the pinch is constant, so one correction finishes it.

**Newton Fractals.** Correction grip, not lifted. The stain is which root the trail approaches. Opens on \(z^3-1\), the cube roots of 1, three still-points, blue, orange, and purple. Dragging a root moves a peg the corrections hunt, and the formula becomes the product once a root leaves the example, as the station does now. The book colours \(z^3+1\) in Figures 4.2.5 and 4.2.6 (PDF Figure 4.3). The line says this picture colours \(z^3-1\). The green plant is the guess; \(0.35+0.45i\) reaches the purple root in 5 steps.

**The Real Line Is a Wall.** The same correction, \(z^2+1\). A plant on the real axis has a correction that stays real, so the trail never finds \(\pm i\). The upper half-plane walks to \(i\). Example 4.2.4 (PDF Example 4.8) and Exercises 1 and 2. The wall is a turn with no imaginary part to spend.

**Which \(c\) Keep 0 Bounded.** The plant is fixed at 0. You only slide the peg. Blue or orange for that one trail is the membership of this \(c\). The set in the book is every peg-position whose fate is blue. Example 4.2.16 (PDF Example 4.11). An attracting fixed point, Definitions 4.2.17 and 4.2.18 and Theorem 4.2.20, is the pinch at a still-point with \(|f'(z_0)| < 1\): the same pinch as the derivative, smaller than 1, so nearby trails are pulled in. For \(z^2+c\) that condition is a cardioid, and the line can say the inequality. This freeze shows one peg at a time. It does not paint the set. The absence of the picture is the second sentence, as the wide review asked.

**Geometric Series.** Stack grip, terms \(z^n\). Inside the unit disk the partial sums crowd at \(1/(1-z)\). Outside, they run. Theorem 4.3.1 (PDF Theorem 4.12). The disk is the disk of the \(c = 0\) freeze, read this time as "the added turns shrink." Marked likely: the strip offers it after the core freezes, not as the next thing the finger must do.

**A Radius, Then a Wall.** The stack around a centre. A circle appears where the added reaches stop shrinking. Inside, the trail crowds. Outside, it runs. Theorems 4.4.1 and 4.4.3 (PDF Theorems 4.15 and 4.16). The same feeling as the unit circle and as the real-line wall: a curve where the hold changes its mind. Marked likely.

**Harmonic.** Not a hold. A readout on the pinch: the height of the real part, the height of the imaginary part, and the check that a small circle around the plant averages to the value at the plant. Theorem 3.3.1 (PDF Theorem 3.8). The Laplacian is zero off the origin because the pinch has no hill. Marked possible. The strip does not offer this as the next freeze. It opens from the pinch, when a learner asks why the height has no local peak. A student who only holds, and only lifts onto the next freeze, finishes on the index.

**The Problem Index (Test 2).** The cards. Opening one sets the plant, the grip, and the step, then pauses. Core cards in map order: sequences, Julia, Newton, then the set of \(c\). Likely and possible cards stay off that path until asked for, as they are now. The two reveals, book and added check, stay independent of the hold.

## What it would cost

One new static page in this repo. No server. KaTeX, the 2D canvas, and Three.js are already vendored. The old stations stay, untouched, and the bottom strip links to them.

Reuse, without rewriting the stations:

- Addresses, powers, roots, and \(\operatorname{Arg}\) from `js/core/complex.js`.
- The orbit walk and the Newton step from `js/core/fractal.js`.
- The off-thread renderer in `js/core/julia-anim-worker.js`, called only after a hold has lasted, for a coarse stain.
- The Riemann-surface geometry the branches station draws, retargeted so the ramp rises in the same frame as the table.
- The 90 ms beat, and the rule that a drag keeps the picture still until release, from the Julia film.

Build:

- One module and one pointer state machine: plant, hold, drag the plant, grab the peg, wind past the seam, pinch. This is the hard part. It is about the size of the Julia film's pointer code, which already taught the lessons this page must not relearn: an off-centre grab must not jump, a release frame must sit, a second gesture must cancel the first, a resize that does not move the view must not cancel the replay.
- One KaTeX line.
- One freeze strip.
- A phone path. One finger plants, holds, and drags. The peg is that same finger after a lift onto the orange point. Pinch is a control that zooms to the square, because a second pointer is unreliable on the phones this site already checks at 390 px. At 390 px the page does not scroll sideways.
- Reduced motion: no replay and no ramp animation. The release is one picture. The line goes straight to the fate.

Leave alone: a bitmap of the set of \(c\); a fifth colour; thread badges on this page; topic chips; any edit to a station module.

The ramp is the piece most likely to become a second viewport. It has to stay dark until the wrist passes the seam, and it has to share the plant with the canvas. A second stage beside the table would be the empty \(c\)-plane the taste log already cut.

## The weakest point

The correction is a different muscle, and the page can only say so. It cannot make the hand discover it.

Square, then slide, is a twist you can mime with nothing in your fingers: double the angle, square the reach, slide by \(c\). Newton's step is a quotient, \(z - p(z)/p'(z)\). Nothing in the wrist produces \(p/p'\). The hold matches. You do not lift, and the colour is where the walk ends. The turn does not match. A student who has just felt \(z^2\) will not feel the correction as the same wrist, because it is not.

If the top line uses one verb for both grips, the page has costumed the correction as a twist. That is the failure the taste log named when a badge pretended to be an idea the picture had not earned. The grip has to change in the sentence, with the formula visible, at the moment the finger is up. The moment that change becomes a toolbar of modes, the one gesture has split into the old map.

There is a smaller crack next to that one. A long hold invites the sharp fractal, and the sharp fractal is the exhibit three batches cut back to a single picture. If the stain fills the table on its own, the finger is a cursor and the gesture is over. The stain stays coarse and late. The existing Julia and Newton stations remain the place a student goes for the sharp picture. A page that cannot bear to be incomplete at the fingertip has already left the hand.
