# Rebuttal B

The equator is the right identification and the wrong front door. A and C both start on the paper the exam is printed on. I started on a sphere whose latitude is log|z|. That makes five disks one hemisphere, and it makes the night before Test 2 harder. I would rather lose the front door than keep pretending the belt is what a tired student can use.

## A — the hand

What A gets right, that the equator misses:

I painted the answer first. The southern hemisphere arrives already blue, the northern already orange, and the trails are polylines computed in one frame. The night before Test 2 the student needs one plant and the refusal to lift, with landings arriving on a beat. Definition 4.1.1 is a tail that eventually sits in a disk. An orbit is that tail. A’s split between finger-memory and table-memory is the split I blurred. The colour is a record of holds you did not sit through. It should arrive late. I opened with the record.

A is also right that “again” is three grips, and that my line — a spiral on the sphere, a slide in the chart, and no other motion — is false.

- Square, then slide, can be mimed: double the angle, square the reach, slide.
- Newton’s step is a quotient, \(z - p(z)/p'(z)\). Nothing in a wrist produces \(p/p'\).
- A series is a stack of bags into a running sum. The geometric remainder happens to be multiplied by \(z\). Theorem 4.1.17 is a subtraction, \(z_n = S_n - S_{n-1}\), and the converse is unclaimed.

I filed every series under the equator because one series is a spiral. That is a trap for the item the book actually prints. A changes the grip in the sentence and leaves the formula visible. I would have costumed the correction as a loxodrome.

Fatal flaw, the night before Test 2: Newton is the long block, and the wrist cannot do it. A builds the page out of the twist anyway, and leaves the sharp Julia and Newton pictures on other stations, behind a freeze strip, with only a coarse stain on the table. The student acquires a habit that misreads the correction, then gets sent back on the tour to see the picture the habit cannot draw. A names this crack — one verb for both grips costumes the correction as a twist — and ships the mime. An hour of turning beats a sentence that says “different muscle.”

Absorb the hold, the beat, and the three grip names, with the stain kept late. Leave the pointer novel. Peg, pinch, wind, and a ramp in the same frame are a second Julia film. The night before cannot afford those manners.

## C — tilt the paper

What C gets right, that the equator misses:

The exam paper is flat, and the circles on it are round. Powers in this course are \(r\) to the \(n\) and angle times \(n\). Circles \(|z - a| = R\) are coins on that paper. My latitude is \(\log|z|\), which straightens the loxodrome and makes the unit circle the only curve that matters. Addition and a disk about \(3 + 4i\) become debris on a chart. C keeps \(r\), and turns \(\log r\) on for one bookmark, the disk at \(c = 0\), where doubling a negative log runs to \(-\infty\). That is the correct scope for my favourite coordinate. I knew addition was the strain. I still made the sphere the room.

C’s four words are the exam’s words: settles, stays, leaves, which. The eighth roots stay and do not settle. A Newton pixel answers which root, a different question from whether the walk leaves. I washed the closed southern hemisphere blue, called it stayed, and borrowed purple for the cycle. Purple already means the third root. The reviews refused that kind of second job. C puts the fine split in the sentence and takes it out of the paint. The walker, the slide \(c\), and a root are different shapes, so orange can stop meaning both “left” and “the handle.” I should have done that before I drew a belt.

The three beats — which paper, how many times, which fate — are the ten-second recognition I never built. The equator reorganizes objects. C reorganizes sentences. The failure at 10pm is grabbing the sequence test for a series, or reading one application of \(f\) as an orbit, or treating bounded as convergent. A sphere does not stop that grab.

Fatal flaw, the night before Test 2: the gesture the pitch is named for turns Test 2 off. Paint is forbidden while the camera is tilted. The helicoid carries one walker and that walker’s footprints. Julia, Newton, the radius, and the half-plane live on the flat camera, as bookmarks. Tilting teaches \(\arg\), \(\operatorname{Arg}\), and the branch cut. That is the staircase station, and it is Test 1. A student who performs the one gesture is looking at the wrong test. The three beats are the right drill, and they sit around a picture the gesture abandons. Retiring both homes that night makes it practical: the stations they already know how to open are no longer the front door, and the new front door hides the basins.

Absorb paper-first, \(r\) rather than \(\log r\) as the default, the four fate words, paint that means fate only, handles as shapes, and a series drawn as two marks with the converse left unclaimed. Leave the helicoid as the space. Height spent on floors cannot also be the multiplier sphere; C already spent it, and correctly refused to spend it again on the 4D graph. Leave the bitmap of the set of \(c\). The cardioid is this disk after \(c = \lambda/2 - \lambda^2/4\). That photograph is the \(|f'| < 1\) fact. The set of every \(c\) whose critical orbit stays is a larger paint, and one orbit at a time is still the honest view of it.

## What I keep, and the revised pitch

The chain-rule product is the part of the equator neither rival has, and it is not a night-before tool. On \(z^2\), the place stays on the unit circle while \(2^n \lambda^{2^n - 1}\) runs to infinity. That is why the colour can change under a nudge exactly on the Julia set of \(c = 0\). It belongs in one sentence on the disk overlay. A permanent inset on every drag is how the page crowds, which I already confessed and then designed anyway.

Revised pitch:

Plant one finger on the exam paper and do not lift. The paper stays flat: radius is \(r\), a circle centred away from the origin stays round, and a slide is a slide. The grip is named before the first landing, and it is one of three: a twist you can mime, a correction \(z - p(z)/p'(z)\) with the formula on the line, or a stack of bags into a partial sum. Landings arrive on a beat. The words under the finger are settles, stays, leaves, or which; the eighth roots stay and do not settle, and a Newton colour is which root. Paint, when it comes, is late and means fate only; the slide and the roots are shapes. When the question is a modulus — the filled disk of \(z^2\), the geometric series, \(|f'| < 1\) — the unit circle is the equator of the sphere this paper charts: inside, the trail falls south, and on the circle the place stays while the chain-rule product runs north. The cardioid is that southern cap after \(c = \lambda/2 - \lambda^2/4\). The sphere is the overlay for that question. It is not the front door.
