# Council — reframe round 1

The student is the one sitting with Howell & Mathews, Chapters 2 to 4, the night before Test 2. Three facts about that student govern every vote below.

Recognition under pressure. The paper is a stack of sentences. The freeze, when it comes, is not a missing room. It is not knowing which fact the sentence is asking for: a power or a square, a term or a partial sum, one application or an orbit, bounded or convergent, which root or whether the walk leaves.

Newton is about a third of the test. The timed plan gives it the same block as the Julia walk. A picture that makes the correction feel like a twist, or like a south pole, fails that third even if the rest of the page is true.

Test 1 to Test 2 is one course. Test 1 taught two addresses, a power, the roots of unity on a circle, and a derivative that is one complex number. Test 2 starts at a point and does a motion again. A front door that retires those stops, or that makes the student learn a sphere before they can say them, spends the night on a new lobby.

The wide review’s sentence stands. A point has two addresses and a function moves the plane. The colour of a start is the fate of doing that motion again. The rebuttals already converged: flat paper first; three grips (a twist \(z^2+c\), Newton’s correction \(p/p'\) written as a formula, a series as a stack); four fate words (settles, stays, leaves, which root); the unit circle as the wall where two trails part at \(c=0\); the multiplier, or the chain-rule product, as a number beside the fate; \(c\) as a notch rather than a fate colour; the sphere or the tilt only when the question is about modulus or \(\operatorname{Arg}\).

What follows is the argument for each revised pitch, then the cross-examination, then the one build.

## A, in the strongest revised form

Plant one finger on the exam paper and do not lift it. The paper stays flat. The first press is once: both addresses are already the plant, one application of the rule, and the pinch is \(f'(z)\) written out. Again is a separate press, capped at eight, and it does not start by itself. An instrument that auto-plays the orbit turns every sentence into an orbit, and a mapping, a derivative, and the first written Newton correction are one application. Example 4.2.1 is five corrections you compute, then the picture.

The grip is named in the sentence, with the formula visible, before the first landing. There are three, and they are different muscles.

- Twist. Square, then slide: \(z^2+c\). This one can be mimed.
- Correction. \(z - p(z)/p'(z)\). Nothing in a wrist produces \(p/p'\). The quotient stays on the line. One verb for both grips would costume the correction as a twist, which is the failure a badge makes when it pretends to be an idea the picture has not earned.
- Stack. The next bag added onto a running sum. A series is two marks, because Theorem 4.1.17 is one direction: if the partial sums settle, the terms go to 0. The converse is unclaimed. One trail cannot show a one-direction theorem.

The words under the finger are the book’s words. Settles means the footprints eventually sit in every disk around a limit (Definition 4.1.1). Stays means they fit in some disk and may never sit down, which is \(((1+i)/\sqrt{2})^n\) and the unit circle under \(z^2\) (Theorem 4.1.8, the converse failing in public). Leaves means the trail is headed for the point at infinity. Newton’s fate is which root. That word is not also the floor and not also the address.

On a pure twist with the slide parked at 0, two trails are drawn, because they are not the same again. The powers \(\lambda^n\) and the squares \(\lambda, \lambda^2, \lambda^4, \ldots\). Inside the unit circle both go to 0. On the circle they part. The product of the pinches along the orbit is a number beside the fate, not a second disk and not a sphere: for \(z^2\) the orbit falls to 0 on \(|z|<1\), while the one-step multiplier \(2z\) lies inside the unit disk only for \(|z|<1/2\). Drop that number and the page teaches a false merger, which is worse than the two maps the site already has.

The slide \(c\) is a notch with no fate colour. A root is a pin. Paint, when it comes, is late and means fate only. The equator and the floors are sentences on this table. The sphere and the helicoid stay the stations they are.

## Cross-examination of A

The night-before student can use the hold, the three names, the fate words, the two trails, and the notch. Three things in this pitch still fail that student.

The correction remains the muscle the page can only name. Square-then-slide can be done with an empty hand. Newton’s step cannot. If the page opens on the twist and sends the sharp Newton picture to another station, the hour of turning builds a habit, and the formula on the line arrives too late. The correction has to be a formula the student can press into, with the book’s five steps from \(1/4+i/4\) to \(i\) on the same paper, not a freeze strip that points down the hall. Newton is a third of the test. A pitch whose honest weak point is “I can only say so” does not get to be the only picture.

The pointer novel is the second failure. Peg, pinch, wind, and a ramp in the same frame are a second Julia film. The reviews already paid for the manners of that film: an off-centre grab must not jump, a release frame must sit, a second gesture must cancel the first. The night before cannot afford to relearn them. The plant is one drag. The notch is not a second handle.

The lift at the end, naming one of thirty-five freezes, is the tour again. This build does not retire the map and does not pretend one page is every stop. It points at the stops the formula already belongs to.

What A keeps, after that cross-examination: the flat table, once against again, the three formulas, the four fate words with “which” narrowed to which root, two trails at \(c=0\), the product as a number, the notch, the stain left off.

## B, in the strongest revised form

The unit circle is one wall. Powers that die, the geometric series, the filled set of \(z^2\), and the place where a multiplier product changes its mind are one equator. Latitude on the sphere the reciprocal station already builds is \(\log|z|\) in the pitch and the stereographic height \((|z|^2-1)/(|z|^2+1)\) in the repo. Those slopes differ. The revised pitch drops the sphere as the front door and keeps the identification.

Plant one finger on the exam paper. The paper stays flat: radius is \(r\), a circle centred away from the origin stays round, and a slide is a slide. \(\log r\) is the right coordinate for one question, the disk at \(c=0\), where doubling a negative log runs to \(-\infty\). It is the wrong default, because addition, a disk about \(3+4i\), and Newton’s subtraction are half of what the student will write.

The chain-rule product is the part neither rival had, and it is a number, not an inset. On \(z^2\), a start on the unit circle can stay there while \(2^n \lambda^{2^n-1}\) runs to infinity. That is why a nudge changes the colour exactly on the Julia set of \(c=0\). For the same map the one-step multiplier is \(2z\), inside the unit disk only for \(|z|<1/2\), while the orbit falls to 0 for every \(|z|<1\). A page that draws only \(\lambda\) against the unit circle merges \(|z|<1\) with \(|f'(z)|<1\). The cardioid is that southern cap after \(c=\lambda/2-\lambda^2/4\). It is a formula. The set of every \(c\) whose critical orbit stays is a larger paint, and one orbit at a time is still the honest view of it.

The hemisphere is the true picture for pure multiplication and for \(z^2\). It is a special case as soon as a slide has bent the belt, and it cannot name three roots. Each simple root of Newton’s map is manufactured so its multiplier is 0. Two poles cannot name three roots. “Blue is south, orange is north” applied to \(z^3-1\) is the wrong reading, and it is the reading a washed sphere invites. So the sphere is the overlay for a modulus question. It is not on the page the night before.

The seam and the real-line wall share a picture and not a definition. The seam is a choice of where longitude jumps. For \(z^2+1\), Newton’s map preserves the real axis because the coefficients are real. A trail that starts there stays there and never reaches \(\pm i\). That sentence has to sit on the correction itself.

## Cross-examination of B

The identification is right and the instrument is the danger. A student with time for one new picture will leave holding the legend of the picture they were shown. If that picture is a belted sphere, the legend is “blue is south, orange is north,” and the Newton block the next morning is three finite attractors, each with multiplier 0, none of them the north pole. Putting the Newton still under the sphere and captioning the mistake does not unload the legend. Leaving the sphere off the page does.

The other half of the cross-examination is the clock. The sentences they must still produce are \(|z_k|=|z_0|^{2^k}\), five Newton steps from \(1/4+i/4\) to \(i\), and the partial sums of a geometric series. A loxodrome, a second sphere for multipliers, and six pictures on a phone do not help those sentences. The product earns its place as a number beside the fate. A permanent inset on every drag is how this site crowds, which B already confessed.

Addition is not a guest. The chart has to be the page, at full size. B’s revised pitch already says this. The build takes that sentence literally: there is no sphere beside the paper.

What B keeps: the unit circle drawn on the flat paper as the wall, the two trails parting on it, the product as a number so \(|z|<1\) is not mistaken for \(|f'|<1\), the cardioid as \(c=\lambda/2-\lambda^2/4\) and not as a bitmap, the real axis named as a formula rather than as a date line.

## C, in the strongest revised form

The exam paper is flat, and the unit circle is drawn on it as the belt where a pure power and a pure square stop sharing a fate. A finger plants and is not lifted. That trail is the sequence. A stain of every other start is allowed only after the trail has spoken, and this build does not ask for the stain.

The three questions, revised so they stop lying, are: which paper, the square grid or the belt; which muscle, twist, Newton correction, or a stack of bags; which fate of the place, settles, stays, leaves, or which root. “Which” as one bucket put the floor, the root, and the address back together. Those were on the list of confusions in order to be separated. The fourth word is which root.

The grips are words said over a printed formula. They are not buttons that invent a picture the formula did not name. The picture shows the muscle the formula already named. If the beats were a toolbar, the gesture would collapse back into the old map, which was A’s failure mode and would be this one too.

Beside the fate sits one number, the modulus of the multiplier product along the trail. \(c=\lambda/2-\lambda^2/4\) is what that number becomes for a fixed point of \(z^2+c\). The seam is a choice of name, and it is the staircase, used when the sentence is about \(\operatorname{Arg}\). The real axis under \(z^2+1\) is a wall the formula forces. Newton is the correction, written out, not mimed.

Colour, if it is painted, means fate and nothing else. Blue stayed. Orange left. A third paint is the third root, and only on a Newton rule. Handles are shapes. A round dot is the plant. A square notch is the slide \(c\). A pin is a root. The orange-handle collision goes away because the notch has no fate colour. “Stays” and “settles” share blue. The word does the fine split. A point of \(((1+i)/\sqrt{2})^n\) stays and does not settle. Painting that distinction with a fifth colour, or borrowing purple from the third root for a cycle, spends the spare colour the reviews already refused.

The stair is the view of \(\arg\), and only then. Height spent on floors cannot also be the multiplier, and it cannot also be the graph of \(f\). The helicoid is not the house.

## Cross-examination of C

The tilt is a motion of the camera. Test 2 is a motion of the point. Paint was forbidden while the camera was tilted, so the gesture the pitch was named for turned Test 2 off. Julia, Newton, the radius, and the half-plane lived on the flat camera as bookmarks. A student who performs that one gesture is looking at Test 1. The staircase station already teaches \(\operatorname{Arg}\), \(\arg\), and the branch cut. Retiring both homes the night before makes it practical in the worst way: the stops they know how to open stop being the front door, and the new front door hides the basins.

The flea inset that was supposed to carry the derivative is the old derivative station docked, not a consequence of the tilt. Chapter 3 does not get easier by being filmed from the side. The unity that survives is one walker and three questions, and the stair is the right paper for only one of the two papers. The build keeps the questions and leaves the camera flat.

Drawing the set of \(c\) in a first build spends the night on a shape the written paper does not ask them to sketch. The question is which \(c\) keep the orbit of 0 bounded, and whether \(|f'(z_0)|<1\). A coarse paint is a picture they will try to remember instead of the inequality. The site’s decision to state the set was about the exam.

What C keeps: paper first, \(r\) rather than \(\log r\), the four fate words, paint that means fate only, handles as shapes, a series as two marks with the converse unclaimed, the belt drawn on the paper, the product as a number, the stair and the sphere left in their stations.

## Where the three still fought

Again, or a beat. B wanted landings on a timer, the 90 ms the Julia film uses, because Definition 4.1.1 is a tail you watch arrive. A and C wanted a press, because an auto-playing orbit erases once. The mapping, the derivative, and the first written correction are once. The vote is the press. Again applies the formula one time, stops at eight, and does not start by itself. Dragging the plant clears the trail back to the plant, so a new start is not born already iterated. The opening example may already show eight steps, because a chip is a jump to a sentence the student has met, and a jump is not a path. The gesture is not the chip.

The product, as a disk or as a number. All three, revised, put it in the sentence. A disk on every drag merges back into the crowding B confessed. The number sits beside the fate: \(|z|\), the one-step \(|f'(z)|\), and the modulus of the product along the steps shown. On \(z^2+c\) the derivative is \(2z\), so the one-step number does not even depend on \(c\), and the fate does. That is the split the student has to be able to say.

The sphere and the tilt. They are true for a modulus question and for an argument question, and they are the wrong front door for this test. The page says so in one sentence and links to the reciprocal stop and the staircase. It does not draw them.

Orange. It already means a walk that left, and on the Newton picture it means the orange root. The reviews refused a fifth colour, and they also refused to pretend the \(c\) handle is not orange on the Julia page. This page does not import that handle. The slide is a square in the ink of the page. Orange on a landing means the walk left. Orange on a pin means that root. The sentence names which of those is on the page. The Julia station keeps its handle until a later batch; the colour key still tells the truth about that station. This station does not add a swatch.

Which root, against a toolbar. The formulas are chips because the exam prints formulas. Pressing one replaces the sentence on the line, and the picture follows that sentence. The gesture stays the plant. A chip that jumped the picture without changing the formula would be the toolbar A and C both rejected.

## The pick

Build the flat paper. One new station. Not a new front door, not a sphere, not a tilt, not a rewrite of the stops.

A wins the hand and loses the correction unless the correction is on the same paper, as a formula, the night before. B wins the wall and the product and loses the moment the sphere becomes the room. C wins the words, the shapes, and the recognition, and loses the moment the camera moves. The synthesis is the revised pitch all three were already standing on, with the fights above called: Again is a press, the product is a number, the notch is ink, Newton is a formula on this paper, and the sphere and the stair stay where they are.

The prototype is that station. It is registered on the Test 2 map and linked from the Test 2 home. Test 1 is untouched.

## The prototype

### The screen

One flat plane, the exam paper, from about \(-2.2\) to \(2.2\) on each axis. The unit circle is a hairline in the ink of the page, labeled “unit circle.” It is not orange and it is not filled. A filled disk would be the fate of every start, and the stain stays off.

The plant is a green round dot, the start, the same green the colour key already uses. The one gesture is dragging it. The plane’s handle keeps the value until the pointer moves, so an off-centre press does not jump. Arrow keys move it. Dragging, or an arrow key, clears the footprints back to the plant.

The square labeled \(c\) is the slide. It is drawn only while the formula is the twist. It is a hollow square in the ink of the page, not a fate colour, and it is not a handle. Chips set it: \(c=0\), \(c=-0.75\), \(c=i\). A chip jumps. It does not film.

Pins are diamonds, drawn only while the formula is a correction. For \(z^2+1\) they are \(i\) in blue and \(-i\) in orange. For \(z^3-1\) they are the three cube roots, blue, orange, and purple, in the order the Newton station already uses. Orange on a pin is that root. It is not \(c\), and it is not an escaped pixel. Purple is the third root and nothing else on this page. The pins do not drag. Dragging a root would change the polynomial the way the Newton station does, and that is a second gesture.

Footprints are the steps Again has applied. A blue dot stayed. The word then says whether it settles or stays. An orange dot left. On a correction, a footprint takes the colour of the root it is walking toward only in the sentence; the dots of a walk that has not yet landed stay in the ink, and the fate line carries the root’s colour. No fractal texture. No fifth colour.

### The one gesture

Drag the green plant.

Again is a button, not the gesture. It applies the formula once. At eight steps it disables and reads “Eight steps shown.” It does not start by itself. Reduced motion has nothing to turn off, because nothing animates.

The formula chips are the exam’s sentences. They are not a mode switch hiding behind the drag.

- Twist: \(z^2+c\). Opens on \((1+i)/\sqrt{2}\) with \(c=0\) and eight steps, because that is where the two trails part.
- Correction: \(z-(z^2+1)/(2z)\). Opens on \(1/4+i/4\) with five steps, Example 4.2.1.
- Correction: \(z-(z^3-1)/(3z^2)\). Opens on \(0.35+0.45i\) with five steps, the guess that reaches the purple root.
- Stack: the bags \(z^n\) and the pile of partial sums. Opens on \((1+i)/2\), which settles at \(1+i\).

### The words on the screen

The line under the picture uses four words, in the book’s senses.

- Settles. Eventually inside every disk around a limit. Definition 4.1.1.
- Stays. Inside some disk, and maybe never sits down. Theorem 4.1.8 says a list that settles is bounded. The cycle is the converse failing.
- Leaves. Out of every disk, toward the point at infinity.
- Which root. The correction’s question. It is not which floor and not which address.

At \(c=0\) the line says both trails. For \((1+i)/\sqrt{2}\): powers stay, the eighth roots, and do not settle; squares settle at 1. Inside the circle, on \(1/2+i/4\), both settle at 0. Outside, both leave.

Beside the fate, three numbers on a twist. \(|z|\). The one-step \(|f'(z)|=|2z|\). The modulus of the product of those derivatives along the steps shown, the chain rule, Equation 3.1.10, applied one step at a time. On \(1/2+i/4\), \(|z|\) is about \(0.559\) and \(|2z|\) is about \(1.118\). The orbit still settles. The product along a few steps falls. On the eighth root, \(|z|=1\) and the product after eight squares is \(2^8=256\), while the place has settled at 1. The cardioid is the sentence \(c=\lambda/2-\lambda^2/4\) for a fixed point with \(|\lambda|<1\). It is not drawn.

On a correction the line names the root, and it says the multiplier at a simple root is 0. A real plant on \(z^2+1\) leaves every disk and never answers which root, because every landing stays real. Example 4.2.4. The seam is named in the same sentence as a different fact: a choice of name, on the staircase, not this wall.

On a stack the line uses two words, one for the bags and one for the pile. Theorem 4.1.17 in one direction. Theorem 4.3.1, marked likely, for the geometric series inside the circle. The number beside them is \(|z|\), labeled as the ratio, not as a derivative.
