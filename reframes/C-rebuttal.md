# Reframer C — Rebuttal

A and B both caught a lie in my three beats. I treated “again” as one press. It is three motions, and two of them part company on the unit circle. My tilt is a motion of the camera. Test 2 is a motion of the point.

## A, the hand

What A gets right, that mine misses: the learner is the finger, and the colour is a memory of fingers that were not this one. I offered a switch called “paint every start.” That switch is the exhibit. The night before, the knowledge is one trail: plant, do not lift, read the landings. The stain of the whole table is what you trust after the trail has already said blue or orange. A also puts the pinch on the fingertip before the first step, a small square already turned by \(f'(z)\). I docked that square in a flea inset and opened it on bookmarks. Newton’s correction and \(|f'(z_0)| < 1\) are that pinch, spent. A student who sees it only after they have chosen the derivative bookmark has already missed the order of the course.

A’s three grips are the correction my second beat needed. Twist is composition: square, then land, then square from there. Stack is a sum: the next bag added onto a running total. Correction is \(z - p(z)/p'(z)\), a quotient, then the same quotient from the new plant. I filed series under “again” next to orbits, and I filed Newton under “again” next to \(z^2\). A is right that those are different muscles, and that a page which uses one verb for them has costumed the correction as a twist.

The fatal flaw, the night before Test 2: the new muscle on that test is the one A cannot mime. Square-then-slide can be done with an empty hand. Newton’s step cannot. Test 2’s core is sequences, the disk and the slide \(c\), and Newton — one step, which root, the real line as a wall. A page whose honest weak point is “the correction is a different muscle, and I can only say so” fails the third of the test that is not a wrist. Choosing the grip “with the hand up, before the plant” is a mode switch. A already says that if the switch becomes a toolbar, the gesture has collapsed back into the old map. The exam is that toolbar, printed as formulas. The student does not get to plant \(z^2\) and hold until the page feels like Newton.

Two sharper cuts. The gesture only exists while the laptop is open. Mine was built for the minute after the laptop shuts, when the sentence is on paper and the hand is holding a pencil. A never leaves the table, and the lift at the end names one of thirty-five freezes, which is the tour again. And the orange peg is the bug the reviews already paid to separate: A lets the slide \(c\) and an escaped trail share orange, with a caption to say which. The night before, the caption loses. The student points at the handle and says a point escaped.

## B, the equator

What B gets right, that mine misses, is the geometry I demoted to an optional overlay. On a stair whose radius is \(r\), the unit circle is one cylinder among many. On the sphere the reciprocal station already builds, it is the equator, and it is where the fates split. For \(z^2\), inside it the place-trail falls to 0, outside it the place-trail runs to \(\infty\), on it the place only turns. The geometric series, \(K_0\), and the power \((1/2 + i/4)^n\) are that southern cap. I knew this and hid it behind a log-radius overlay on one bookmark, because I was busy defending \(r\) against \(\log r\) so that circles \(|z - a| = R\) would stay round. The belt can be drawn on the flat paper. I did not need to change the coordinate to put it in the centre.

The fact I did not have at all: the same number runs two trails, and they split on that belt. \(\lambda^n\) at \(\lambda = (1+i)/\sqrt{2}\) cycles the eighth roots and does not settle. The squaring orbit of that same point goes to \(i\), then to \(-1\), then to \(1\), and stays. My beats would have tagged both “stair, again, stays.” That is the spine’s sentence, and it is false in the way that costs marks. Off the belt the two trails share a fate and differ in speed. On the belt they do not. A night-before page that cannot show both has glued Chapter 4.1 to the Julia orbit.

Worse, and also B’s: \(|z| < 1\) and \(|f'(z)| < 1\) are different points. For \(z^2\) the single-step multiplier is \(2z\), inside the unit disk only for \(|z| < 1/2\), while the orbit falls to 0 for every \(|z| < 1\). The product \((f^{\circ n})'(z_0) = f'(z_0)\cdots f'(z_{n-1})\) is what lines up with the hemisphere. My fate words describe the place only. A student who leaves my page will hear “inside the disk” and paint the attracting-fixed-point cardioid with the same disk. The cardioid is that southern hemisphere after \(c = \lambda/2 - \lambda^2/4\), a photograph of one region, not a second idea. The set of \(c\) whose orbit of 0 stays bounded is larger than the photograph. I proposed to paint that larger set first. The formula is the one that matches the theorem they can be asked to use.

B also catches a sentence of mine that is simply wrong. I said the branch cut and the real-line wall were the same question asked of a line of walkers. The seam is a choice of where longitude jumps. For \(z^2 + 1\), Newton’s map preserves the real axis because the coefficients are real. A trail that starts there stays there and never reaches \(\pm i\). That is the formula, not a date line. Pinning them on one cliff teaches a false sameness the half-plane exercise is written to test.

The fatal flaw, the night before Test 2: the moral of B’s sphere sits one step past the paper, and the two poles cannot name the picture that is on the paper. The load-bearing inset is the multiplier product. Drop it and the page merges \(|z| < 1\) with \(|f'| < 1\), which B correctly calls worse than the two maps we have. Keep it and the student spends the night on a chain rule for iterates, loxodromes, and a second sphere, while the sentences they must still produce are \(|z_k| = |z_0|^{2^k}\), five Newton steps from \(1/4 + i/4\) to \(i\), and the partial sums of a geometric series. Newton is the break B admits. Each simple root is a manufactured south pole, and there are three of them. “Blue is south, orange is north” applied to \(z^3 - 1\) is the wrong reading, and it is the reading the picture invites. Slides — the \(+c\), the partial sums, Newton’s subtraction — are half of Chapter 4, and a beautiful sphere makes them look like the chart underneath. B says the chart must be the same size. The thrill will not obey that instruction. Six pictures on a phone the night before is a crowd, and B already knows it.

A smaller crack, so the big one stays the big one: B’s latitude is \(\log|z|\) in the pitch and the repo’s stereographic height \((|z|^2 - 1)/(|z|^2 + 1)\) in the build. The equator matches. The height the student would point at does not. A loxodrome and that coordinate are not the same slope.

## What I should absorb

From A, absorb the three muscles and the order of attention. Do not absorb the pointer state machine, the shared orange, or the refusal to draw a sentence the student can say after the laptop shuts. From B, absorb the belt, the two trails, and the multiplier product as a number. Do not absorb the sphere as the front door, a second sphere as an object on the page, purple as a third fate for the cycle, or a live fractal. The stair stays the view of \(\arg\), used when the sentence is about names. It is no longer the house everything lives in.

The grips must not become a toolbar. That was A’s failure mode, and it would be mine if the beats were buttons that change the picture. They are words said over a printed formula. The picture shows the muscle the formula already named.

Revised pitch. The exam paper stays flat, and the unit circle is drawn on it as the belt where a pure power and a pure square stop sharing a fate. A finger plants and is not lifted; that trail is the sequence, and a stain of every other start is allowed only after the trail has spoken. Tilting the paper shows the wrist’s extra turns, and only when the sentence is about \(\operatorname{Arg}\). The three questions are now: which paper, the square grid or the belt; which muscle, twist, Newton correction, or a stack of bags; which fate of the place, settles, stays, leaves, or which root. Beside the fate sits one number, the modulus of the multiplier product along the trail, so \(|z| < 1\) is not mistaken for \(|f'| < 1\), and \(c = \lambda/2 - \lambda^2/4\) is what that number becomes for \(z^2 + c\). The seam is a choice of name. The real axis under \(z^2 + 1\) is a wall the formula forces. Newton is the correction, written out, not mimed.
