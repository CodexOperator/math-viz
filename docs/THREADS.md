# Candidate unifying threads (complex numbers, Chapters 1–3)

The Unifier tests each candidate against every review problem. A thread earns its place only
if it makes specific problems feel obvious. `docs/STORY.md` holds the threads that won.

- **One point, two addresses.** Cartesian (x, y) is where it sits; polar (r, θ) is how to get
  there by turning and walking. The point never changes; convert at will.
- **Slide vs spin-and-stretch.** Adding is a slide (easy in Cartesian); multiplying is a
  spin-and-stretch (easy in polar); conjugation is a mirror; 1/z inverts the radius and mirrors;
  dividing unspins and shrinks; |z|² = z·z̄ because the spins cancel.
- **arg is a staircase, Arg is home.** arg z is the spiral staircase of angles θ + 2πk; Arg z is
  the step we call home. Roots divide the staircase into n distinct points, branches choose a step
  continuously, branch cuts are where the choice must jump, Riemann surfaces make the staircase
  literal.
- **A function is a motion of the plane; time is the parameter.** A brush stroke is a curve
  z(t), and its image f(z(t)) is drawn by the same clock; a morph (1 − s)z + s·f(z) shows the
  plane flowing from input to output.
- **Circles are polar's native shape.** |z − a| = r, roots sit on circles, 1/z sends generalized
  circles to generalized circles, the Riemann sphere turns lines into circles through ∞.
- **The derivative is a local spin-and-stretch.** Cauchy–Riemann says the local linear map has
  the shape [[a, −b], [b, a]]. z̄, Re z and |z|² fail because they flip or squash; a limit must
  agree from every direction of approach.
- **The 4D graph glimpsed through shadows.** Domain colouring (hue = arg, brightness = modulus),
  the modulus landscape, Re and Im surfaces as two projections of one surface in ℝ⁴ (rotate
  between them), the Riemann surface of a root.
