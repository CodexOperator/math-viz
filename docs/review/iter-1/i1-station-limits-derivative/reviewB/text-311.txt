The Derivative Is a Move

Put z0 under a microscope. Close up, a differentiable f turns the ring on the left into a ring on the right, spun and scaled by one number, f′(z0). The lower plane plots the difference quotient for every direction: a circle with centre A and radius |B|. The derivative exists exactly when that circle is a single point.

Same scale as the left plane, centred on f(z0), so the size of this ring compared with the left one is the stretch. Dashed: what a pure A·h + B·conj(h) would do.

Difference quotients: Δw/Δz, one dot per direction

Each dot is (f(z0 + h) − f(z0))/h for one direction. The dashed circle has centre A and radius |B|.

Close up, Δw ≈ A·Δz + B·conj(Δz): a spin-and-stretch plus a mirrored spin-and-stretch. The quotient is A + B·e−2iφ, so when the direction goes round once the dot goes round twice, the other way. Cauchy–Riemann says B = 0.
